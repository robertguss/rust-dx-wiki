In general, optimizations take place within each codegen unit, and then they're linked together at the end.\n\nLTO controls the set of optimizations that LLVM will make during that link-time — for example, inlining or optimization across codegen units.\n\nCargo (via\n\n```\nrustc\n```\n\n) exposes a few options for LTO:\n\n* Off — all LTO disabled\n* \"thin\" LTO — in theory, similar performance benefits to \"fat\" LTO, but less expensive to run\n* \"fat\" LTO — maximum amount of LTO, across all crates at the same time\n\nAnd if the LTO option is not specified,\n\n```\nrustc\n```\n\nuses \"thin local LTO\", which limits \"thin\" LTO only to a single crate at\na time.\n\n### What are the current settings\n\nTurns out that a few years back, I had set\n\n```\nlto = \"thin\"\n```\n\nin my\n\n```\nCargo.toml\n```\n\n:\n\n```\n```\n[profile.release]\nlto = \"thin\"\ndebug = \"full\"\n```\n```\n\nAnd, while we're at it,\n\n```\ndebug = \"full\"\n```\n\nenables all debug symbols (where they'd normally be excluded by default for the\n\n```\nrelease\n```\n\nprofile).
